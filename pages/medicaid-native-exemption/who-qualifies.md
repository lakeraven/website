# Who Qualifies as "Indian" for the Medicaid Exemption

<p class="usa-intro">The exemption is broader than tribal enrollment.</p>

*Last reviewed October 2026. Part of our guide to the [American Indian and Alaska Native exemption](/medicaid-native-exemption/). Not legal advice.*

---

The federal rule excludes from Medicaid work requirements anyone who "meets the definition of Indian at § 447.51" ([42 CFR 435.554(c)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-435.554)). That definition draws on the Indian Health Care Improvement Act ([25 U.S.C. 1603](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/25/1603) and [1679](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/25/1679)) and on IHS eligibility rules ([42 CFR 136.12](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-136.12)).

A person qualifies if **any one** of the following is true ([42 CFR 447.51](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-447.51)).

---

## 1. Members of federally recognized tribes

Anyone who "is a member of a Federally-recognized Indian tribe." Tribes decide their own membership.

## 2. Urban Indians

A person who lives in an urban center and meets one or more of these:

- Is a member of a tribe, band or other organized group of Indians. That includes groups terminated since 1940 and groups recognized by the state where the person lives, not only federally recognized tribes.
- Is a **descendant, in the first or second degree,** of such a member. In practice, that means the child or grandchild of a member.
- Is an Eskimo, Aleut or other Alaska Native.
- Is considered an Indian by the Secretary of the Interior for any purpose, or is determined to be an Indian under the Secretary's regulations.

## 3. People the Secretary of the Interior considers Indian

Anyone the Secretary of the Interior considers to be an Indian for any purpose, wherever they live.

## 4. People eligible for Indian health services

Anyone "considered by the Secretary of Health and Human Services to be an Indian for purposes of eligibility for Indian health care services, including as a California Indian, Eskimo, Aleut, or other Alaska Native." The definition also reaches anyone determined eligible under [42 CFR 136.12](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-136.12), the IHS eligibility rule.

---

## Groups most often missed

**California Indians.** California has many people who descend from California tribes but are not enrolled in a federally recognized tribe. They are named in the definition, but state guidance often doesn't mention them.

**Urban Indians and descendants.** The urban prong covers children and grandchildren of members, and members of state-recognized and terminated tribes. A state that says only "tribal member" can leave these people out.

**People served by IHS or tribal clinics.** If IHS has found a person eligible for Indian health services, that person meets the definition, even if they aren't enrolled.

---

## Race is not the test

The definition turns on political status, membership, descent and IHS eligibility, not on race. A person who checks "American Indian or Alaska Native" on a race question has not necessarily shown they qualify, and a person who qualifies may not check that box. State forms that rely on the race question alone can both miss eligible people and mark others incorrectly.
