# Medicaid Work Requirements: The American Indian and Alaska Native Exemption

<p class="usa-intro">Who is exempt, how states must verify it, and what tribes and Indian health programs can do now.</p>

*Last reviewed October 2026. This guide explains federal rules in plain language. It is not legal advice.*

---

## The short version

H.R. 1, the 2025 budget reconciliation law, requires most adults in the Medicaid expansion group to show 80 hours a month of work or other "community engagement" to keep their coverage. In most states this starts on **January 1, 2027**.

American Indians and Alaska Natives are excluded. Under the federal rule, anyone who meets the Medicaid definition of "Indian" is a *specified excluded individual*, and the work requirement does not apply to them ([42 CFR 435.554(c)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-435.554)).

That definition is broader than tribal enrollment. It also covers many urban Indians and their descendants, California Indians, Alaska Natives, and anyone IHS has found eligible for Indian health services. See [Who qualifies](/medicaid-native-exemption/who-qualifies/).

The exclusion only helps if the state recognizes it. Federal rules require states to check the information they already hold first, to enroll promptly while they confirm an exclusion, and not to recheck a person's excluded status between scheduled renewals. See [How states must verify the exemption](/medicaid-native-exemption/verification/).

---

## Why this matters

People lose coverage through process, not only through eligibility. An eligible tribal citizen can still be dropped if the state's application never asks the right question, asks for a document the person doesn't have, or rechecks a status that was already confirmed.

A state's published guidance often uses a short label like "American Indian or Alaska Native" or "tribal member" without saying who that includes. When the label is narrower than the federal definition, people who should be excluded can end up subject to the work requirement.

## What the federal rule says

| Rule | What it requires | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Who is excluded | Anyone who meets the definition of Indian at 42 CFR 447.51 | [435.554(c)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-435.554) |
| Who counts as Indian | Tribal members, many urban Indians and descendants, California Indians, Alaska Natives, and people IHS has found eligible | [447.51](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-447.51) |
| Use data first | States must use reliable information they already have before asking the person for more | [435.557(b), (c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-435.557) |
| Enroll, then confirm | If more information is needed to confirm an exclusion, enroll the person promptly and verify afterward | [435.557(c)(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-435.557) |
| Documents | Before January 1, 2028, a state may accept other information in place of documents. Coverage can't be denied for lack of a document that doesn't exist or isn't reasonably available. | [435.557(b)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-435.557) |
| No rechecking | A state may not reverify excluded status between scheduled renewals unless it learns the status changed | [435.557(d)(4)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-435.557) |

The rule was published at 91 FR 33469 (June 3, 2026) and amended at 91 FR 39028 (June 29, 2026).

## What tribes and Indian health programs can do now

- **Read your state's guidance against the federal definition.** Check whether it names urban Indians, descendants, California Indians and IHS eligibility, or only says "tribal member."
- **Ask how the state will confirm status.** Ask which data it checks first, whether it will accept confirmation from a tribe or an Indian health program, and what a person can provide if they have no enrollment card.
- **Ask whether status will be rechecked.** Federal rules bar rechecking between renewals. Guidance that says exemptions are "verified every 6 months" should be raised with the state.
- **Use tribal consultation.** States with Indian health programs must seek their advice on Medicaid changes that affect them ([Social Security Act 1902(a)(73)](https://www.ssa.gov/OP_Home/ssact/title19/1902.htm)). Exemption wording and verification steps are fair topics.
