# How scorecard review works

Anyone can send evidence that a state's published policy has changed: a state Medicaid agency, a tribe, an Indian Health Service, tribal or urban Indian program, or a member of the public.

## What to send

- A public link to the published policy, such as a state web page, manual section, bulletin, notice, state plan amendment, or waiver document.
- Which requirement it addresses.
- Who you are sending on behalf of.

Please send links, not attachments. Grades rest only on policy anyone can read.

## What happens next

1. We acknowledge your submission by email.
2. The state's page shows **Evidence under review**. The grade does not change yet.
3. Within 10 business days, we read the source, quote it, and decide whether it changes any finding.
4. Evidence from tribes and Indian health programs carries the same weight as evidence from the state.
5. If the grade goes **up**, we publish the change right away.
6. If the grade goes **down**, we tell the state agency first and give it 10 days to respond before the change is published.
7. Every change is listed in the state's history with the date, the old and new grade, and the source.
8. We email you the outcome.

## What a grade does and does not mean

- A grade measures a state's **published** policy against the federal rules for American Indians and Alaska Natives.
- It does not measure how individual cases are handled.
- Policy a state has not published cannot earn credit.
