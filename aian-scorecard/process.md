# How scorecard review works

Anyone can send evidence that a state's published policy has changed, or that we got a finding wrong: a state Medicaid agency, a tribe, an Indian Health Service, tribal or urban Indian program, or a member of the public.

## What to send

Use the [evidence form]({{ aianScorecard.rubric.submission.form_url }}). Each state's page also links to it as **Send a published policy link**, with the state and requirement already filled in. You don't need a Google account. The form asks for:

- A public link to the published policy, such as a state web page, manual section, bulletin, notice, state plan amendment, or waiver document.
- Which requirement it addresses.
- Who you are sending on behalf of, and whether we may name your organization.

Please send links, not attachments. Grades rest only on policy anyone can read.

## If there's any question, we round down

- A requirement is **met** only when the state's published words clearly commit to it.
- It is **contradicted** only when the state's published words clearly apply to American Indians and Alaska Natives and conflict with the federal rule.
- Anything unclear, hedged (such as "may" or "some adults"), or only implied counts as **not published**. It earns no credit and is not called a contradiction.
- We don't adjudicate what a state meant, and we don't weigh arguments about how to read its current wording. If you think a finding is wrong, the fix is for the state to clarify its own published policy. Send us the link and we'll update the grade.

## What happens next

1. The form emails you a copy of your submission.
2. The state's page shows **Evidence under review**. The grade does not change yet.
3. Within 10 business days, we read the source, quote it, and apply the round-down rule above to decide whether it changes any finding.
4. Evidence from tribes and Indian health programs carries the same weight as evidence from the state.
5. If the grade goes **up**, we publish the change right away.
6. If the grade goes **down**, we tell the state agency first and give it 10 days to respond before the change is published. This applies to findings changed by a submission. When we change how we grade, the change applies to every state at once and publishes right away; each affected state's history says what changed and why.
7. Every change is listed in the state's history with the date, the old and new grade, and the source.
8. We email you the outcome.

## What a grade does and does not mean

- A grade measures a state's **published** policy against the federal rules for American Indians and Alaska Natives.
- It does not measure how individual cases are handled.
- Policy a state has not published cannot earn credit.
